This review will be different. It will be live, like programming in Lisp. I will add quotes I found notable, useful, or remember-worthy here. Not all, mind, just the ones I think deserve a space in a reflective review. That’s to say, something to remind me of the key, useful ideas of the book when I re-read this review some time later.

This is why the book has no rating, but a ’live review’ symbol (->->->). So this differs in several ways:

  • I would not live-review a book if it wasn’t worth reading.
  • Placing a subjective rating for a live-reviewed book doesn’t make sense. Let the reader decide!
  • Live-reviews are long! It’s a sort of interactive reflection by me, against quoted material from the text.
  • Books that are live-reviewed, are books I wish to read again to absorb by osmosis.
  • Because this is extremely time-intensive, only a sample handful of such reviews will be offered for free.
  • The value proposition for the paid ones is that
    • first off, the reader saves a lot of time discovering a gem;
    • secondly, the reader gets a much better understanding of the text, a sort of deep preview which will aid in memorization and understanding;
    • third, the reader can see how my highlights differ from their own. A lot can be learned from this as well.
    • Fourth, greenhorn readers would gain a lot of implicitly-impartable critical thinking and reading experience skill points in my reactions to the selections from the live-reviewed gem of a book. And so forth
